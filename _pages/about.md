I am a final-year PhD candidate in computational psychiatry at the [Laureate Institute for Brain Research](https://www.laureateinstitute.org/), advised by [Dr. Ryan Smith](https://www.laureateinstitute.org/ryan-smith.html), and expect to complete my [doctorate](https://sites.utulsa.edu/cyber-students/marishka-mehta/) in **December 2026**. My research uses computational models to understand how people make decisions and stay motivated, and how these processes differ across mental health disorders. I focus on measures that are reliable, generalize to new people, and can predict clinically meaningful differences, such as a person's diagnosis.

I work with behavioral and brain data, using reinforcement-learning and Bayesian models to estimate cognitive processes that cannot be observed directly, such as uncertainty, avoidance, and reward sensitivity. My dissertation focuses on two areas in particular: planning and interoception, the perception of internal bodily signals such as heartbeat and breathing. My broader work spans motivation, self-agency, outcome processing, and digital phenotyping, using 3T and 7T fMRI, clinical measures, and real-world digital data.

Before my PhD, I completed my Master's thesis with [Dr. Laurel Morris](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/people/laurel-morris) at the [Depression and Anxiety Center](https://icahn.mssm.edu/research/depression-anxiety-center), Mount Sinai, where I later worked as a Clinical Research Coordinator. My training spans six institutions on three continents, including a dual Master's in Neuroscience through the [Neurasmus](https://www.neurasmus.u-bordeaux.fr) program.
